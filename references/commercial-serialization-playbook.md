# Commercial Serialization Playbook

Use this reference when the user wants service-level, paid, commercial, launch-ready Korean webnovel progression design. The goal is not only a coherent growth curve; the goal is a growth engine that helps readers click, continue, pay, and stay.

## Commercial Core

Every protagonist growth curve must answer five business-facing story questions:

| Layer | Question | Growth-Curve Requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Click promise | Why does the reader start? | The protagonist type and advantage must be legible from title/blurb/episode 1 |
| Early proof | Why does the reader trust the promise? | Episode 1-3 must show the signature advantage solving a concrete problem |
| Repeatable pleasure | Why does the reader continue free episodes? | Episodes 4-10 must repeat the core reward with variation and reaction |
| Paid conversion | Why does the reader pay now? | Episodes 19-25 must open high-value unresolved reward, not close the main value |
| Retention after payment | Why does the reader keep paying? | The next ceiling, institution reaction, and reward ladder must be visible |

Do not treat monetization as a marketing layer added after plotting. Paid readiness must be baked into the protagonist's growth, visibility, and reward timing.

## Commercial Mode Intake

When context is missing, infer or ask internally from the premise:

| Field | Needed Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Shelf | hunter, tower, academy, murim, rofan, chaebol, idol, sports, cooking, survival, etc. |
| Paid fantasy | what the reader repeatedly buys: domination, revenge, survival relief, rank rise, wealth, romance power, public recognition |
| Episode boundary | launch/free range, paid conversion range, current draft range |
| Protagonist engine | origin + advantage + position + deficiency |
| Visible proof | the scene that proves the premise by episode 3 |
| Repeatable unit | the event type that can recur every 2-3 episodes without feeling identical |
| Conversion asset | the reward/opportunity held open at the paid boundary |

If no platform is specified, assume platform-neutral Korean webnovel pacing: fast hook, concrete first proof, frequent micro-rewards, strong 20-25 episode conversion pressure.

## First 25 Episode Commercial Ladder

| Range | Commercial Job | Growth Job | Required Reader Feeling | Strong Tools |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make the click feel correct | Show wound and signature exception | "This is the promised story" | reversal, rank mismatch, death flag, public insult, impossible test |
| 2-3 | Prove repeatability | Show first use and first limit | "This advantage works" | second proof, cost, witness, measurable gain |
| 4-7 | Stabilize loop | Repeat core pleasure with variation | "I know what I am reading for" | reward rotation, small rival, visible asset |
| 8-10 | Add social reaction | Make the world notice | "The board is changing" | rumor, invitation, contract, rank, clip, guild interest |
| 11-15 | Deepen attachment | Tie reward to ally, rival, or institution | "This win matters beyond numbers" | team risk, family pressure, public duel, audit |
| 16-18 | Seed conversion value | Show a desirable reward not yet obtained | "I want that next thing" | forbidden zone, title, job, artifact, role, company, contract |
| 19-25 | Convert | Deliver partial payoff and open bigger unresolved value | "I need the next paid episode" | irreversible status shift, enemy declaration, cliff reward, new ceiling |

## Paid Boundary Patterns

| Pattern | Works For | Boundary Move | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public rank contradiction | hunter, academy, murim | protagonist wins publicly, but official system refuses/contests the result | if the contest feels like bureaucracy only |
| Ownership just out of reach | chaebol, management, crafting | protagonist earns asset access, but control requires one more dangerous move | if money already solved everything |
| Death flag inverted | villainess, extra, survival | protagonist avoids death, but the avoided route creates a new accusation | if survival feels reset to zero |
| Forbidden zone invitation | tower, murim, fantasy | protagonist earns access to a place readers want to see | if no personal stake follows |
| Rival learns the truth | all genres | rival sees enough to adapt, recruit, blackmail, or counter | if rival becomes omniscient |
| Team consequence | sports, idol, raid | protagonist's growth saves team but creates dependency or resentment | if team members are passive |
| Secret becomes rumor | hidden power genres | not full reveal, but enough rumor to change social pressure | if secrecy never costs anything |
| Contract trap | rofan, guild, agency, chaebol | protagonist wins a contract with dangerous clauses | if legal stakes are too abstract |

## Churn Prevention After First Paid Reward

After the first paid payoff, immediately reveal the next purchase reason:

| After Reader Gets | Add Next |
| --- | --- |
| first public win | institutional reaction and new rank ceiling |
| first revenge payoff | bigger culprit and moral price |
| first asset | control problem, governance, or theft risk |
| first romance trust | political/family cost of that trust |
| first skill unlock | counter, cooldown, or forbidden upgrade path |
| first team victory | teammate injury, dependency, or rival team's adaptation |
| first survival safety | scarcity, refugees, law, or predator group |

Never let the paid boundary fully resolve the most marketable desire. Resolve one promised value and open a higher-value desire.

## Commercial Growth Curve By Reader Fantasy

| Reader Fantasy | Growth Curve | Reward That Sells | Cost That Preserves Tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domination | early burst then public pressure | overpowering public reversal | fear, law, ownership attempts |
| Revenge | delayed reward with irreversible payoffs | betrayer exposed, ruined, or forced to kneel | collateral, moral stain, larger enemy |
| Survival relief | safety accumulation | permanent safety asset | dependency, scarcity, new responsibility |
| Rank rise | staircase with public markers | rank, title, exam, duel, promotion | expectation, audits, rivals |
| Wealth/power | compound accumulation | shares, territory, contract, facility | governance, regulation, liquidity |
| Recognition | reputation reversal | witness, clip, award, public apology | scrutiny, anti-fans, old evidence |
| Romance power | social ascent | trust, contract, household authority | family politics, vulnerability |
| Craft mastery | compound production | product, recipe, artifact, patent | supply, copycats, liability |

## Service-Level Output Requirements

For commercial-grade answers, include:

1. Positioning-grade protagonist diagnosis.
2. Reader purchase fantasy.
3. First 25 episode growth/conversion ladder.
4. Reward calendar by capability, status, resources, burden.
5. Paid-boundary hook.
6. Post-payment retention plan.
7. Churn risks and minimum repairs.
8. Commercial score or readiness verdict.

Use compact tables. Avoid vague praise. Treat the user's premise as a product that must survive paid serialization.

## Commercial Readiness Score

| Category | Points | Pass Standard |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Click clarity | 15 | Protagonist identity and advantage are legible in one sentence |
| Episode 1-3 proof | 15 | The advantage solves a concrete visible problem |
| Repeatable loop | 15 | The core pleasure can recur every 2-3 episodes with variation |
| Reward economy | 15 | Rewards rotate and become more valuable without collapsing tension |
| Paid boundary | 20 | Episode 19-25 creates high-value unresolved desire |
| Post-paid retention | 10 | Next ceiling and next reward ladder are visible after payment |
| Market risk control | 10 | The biggest churn risks have concrete repairs |

Verdict:

- 90-100: Launch-ready growth engine.
- 80-89: Commercially promising; fix the highest churn risk.
- 70-79: Usable draft, not yet strong enough to sell confidently.
- Below 70: Redesign the protagonist engine or first 25 episodes.

## Churn Risk Diagnostics

| Churn Signal | Commercial Meaning | Repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Episode 1 explains but does not prove | readers may not trust the promise | add a concrete reversal scene |
| Episode 3 still has no signature advantage | weak continuation reason | force first tool/knowledge/power use |
| Episode 10 has no social reaction | world feels static | add rumor, contract, rank, witness, or institutional notice |
| Paid boundary resolves the current desire | no reason to pay next | close one door and open a more valuable one |
| All rewards are private | low shareability and weak public tension | add witnesses and visible markers |
| Protagonist only gets stronger | tension collapses | add exposure, control, law, scarcity, obligation |
| Rival is humiliated too early | no adaptive pressure | keep rival's status, information, or institution alive |
| Middle arc lacks asset progression | readers feel drift | add territory, title, company, team, facility, role, or contract |
