# Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to score, compare, or self-check a Korean webnovel protagonist growth curve before delivery. It is especially useful for long-series plans, paid-conversion arcs, or repairs to overpowered protagonists.

## 100-Point Growth Curve Score

| Category | Points | What To Check |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Reader promise clarity | 15 | The first 1-3 episodes clearly show what fantasy the protagonist delivers |
| Growth visibility | 15 | Growth changes scenes, options, public reaction, or access instead of only numbers |
| Reward variety | 15 | Rewards rotate across capability, status, resources, and burden relief |
| Cost and counterplay | 15 | Major wins create price, reaction, limitation, or new vulnerability |
| Position emphasis | 15 | The protagonist's world-position is clear and changes by arc |
| Next ceiling | 10 | The next limit is seeded before the current one breaks |
| Rival/institution adaptation | 10 | Rivals, guilds, sects, agencies, families, markets, or courts learn and react |
| Serialization rhythm | 5 | Micro rewards, arc rewards, and cliff pressure are paced for binge reading |

Score bands:

- 90-100: Strong commercial progression engine. Preserve the core and tune details.
- 75-89: Usable, but one curve or pressure source needs sharpening.
- 60-74: The premise works, but rewards or crises will repeat quickly.
- Below 60: Redesign the protagonist engine before expanding the plot.

## Fast Self-Check

Ask these before finalizing a design:

| Question | Good Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What does the reader want to see again by episode 3? | A repeatable pleasure, not just a premise explanation |
| What improves when the protagonist wins? | One or more of capability, status, resources, burden relief |
| What gets worse or more dangerous after the win? | Exposure, obligation, scarcity, rival data, law, injury, rumor, debt |
| Who notices the growth? | Witness, rival, institution, public, family, sponsor, enemy |
| What can the protagonist still not solve? | A clear next ceiling that is not just "stronger enemy" |
| What reward should be delayed? | The reward that would end the main tension too early |

## Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every reward is a new skill | Power list bloats and scenes feel samey | Replace next skill with title, asset, ally, or public witness |
| Every enemy is just stronger | Scale rises but conflict does not deepen | Add law, reputation, hostages, contracts, terrain, or legitimacy |
| Future knowledge is always correct | Regression becomes a walkthrough | Break prices, loyalty, timing, injuries, and hidden variables |
| Public opinion flips instantly | Reputation arcs lose satisfaction | Use one witness, then small group, then institution, then public myth |
| The protagonist never pays | Wins feel weightless | Add cooldown, debt, suspicion, injury, promise, or moral stain |
| The protagonist only suffers | Reader reward disappears | Add permanent safety, leverage, or visible improvement every few episodes |
| Rivals do not adapt | Antagonists feel fake | Let rivals learn one correct fact and make one wrong inference |
| The next ceiling appears suddenly | Escalation feels arbitrary | Seed rumors, records, failed veterans, forbidden zones, or prices |

## Paid-Conversion Check

For a 20-25 episode free-to-paid opening, verify:

| Check | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Episode 1 | Shows wound and signature exception |
| Episode 3 | Gives a concrete win or survival proof |
| Episode 5 | Reveals limitation, cost, or dangerous witness |
| Episode 10 | Repeats core pleasure in a different situation |
| Episode 15 | Creates public consequence or institutional interest |
| Episode 20-25 | Delivers irreversible status shift and opens larger board |

If the paid-conversion turn is weak, do not add exposition. Add one irreversible change: rank, contract, duel result, public clip, audit, invitation, territory, office, or enemy humiliation.

## Position Emphasis Audit

| Current Position | Needed Arc Movement |
| --- | --- |
| Hidden variable | A dangerous witness or partial reveal |
| Public threat | Legitimacy, control, or law pressure |
| Survival weakling | Permanent safety asset |
| Market disruptor | Regulation, copycat, or governance |
| Defective genius | Cost of talent and public proof |
| Villain reputation | One credible witness and one unresolved victim |
| Team amplifier | Ally success plus responsibility for ally risk |
| Authority figure | Casualty, tax, delegation, or succession pressure |
